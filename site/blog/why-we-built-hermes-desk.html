Why I built Hermes Desk

Trần Việt Tùng, founder · 8 October 2026

I work as a coordinator for real estate sales in Vietnam. My day is one long chat. Buyers ask about units, sales agents ask me to send deposit bills, and when something goes wrong, the complaint lands in the same inbox. From 8 in the morning to 9 at night, that inbox does not stop.

On a busy day I handle 20 to 30 new leads on my own. The hard part is not any single message. It is that three different kinds of work arrive mixed together, and each one needs a different person to act on it.

What goes wrong in a busy inbox

When the inbox gets busy, the same three things keep happening. A hot lead waits an hour because I am chasing a bill. An angry buyer waits a day because their message scrolled out of sight. A bill gets forgotten because nobody owned it.

None of these are hard problems on their own. They are sorting problems. Someone has to read every message, decide what it is, decide who should act, and reply in a way that keeps the customer calm.

What Hermes Desk does

Hermes Desk does that sorting. It reads each message from Zalo, Messenger or email, decides whether it is a lead, a complaint or a billing request, marks how urgent it is, and routes it to the right person. Then it drafts a reply in Vietnamese, in the tone I would use.

It never sends anything by itself. I read the draft, change it if I need to, and approve it. That rule matters. In this job, a wrong price or a wrong promise to a buyer costs real money.

Two months in

For the past two months, I and a group of 10 coordinators from my network have used it every day. Each of us handles 15 to 20 leads a day. The pilot users report that about 90% of drafts go out as written or with light edits, that they respond to and handle leads about 70% faster, and that they miss about 90% fewer leads than before.

These are our own numbers, measured by the people doing the work. The next step is to measure them inside the product.

Why Claude

Customer messages in Vietnamese are casual, short and often half-written. Claude handles that well, and it writes replies that sound polite without sounding like a robot. The prototype runs its sorting and drafting on Claude today, and I design and build the rest of Hermes Desk with Claude. Inside my own tool, the few contacts that no rule covers are still classified by Gemini Flash. Moving that step onto Claude is next. You can try the sorting step yourself in the live demo.
